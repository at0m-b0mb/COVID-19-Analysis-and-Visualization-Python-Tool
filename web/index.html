How to read this

Reported, not actual

Every number here is a reported case or death. Testing capacity, reporting rules and backlogs differ by country and changed over the three years, so these counts are a record of what was detected and filed — a floor, not a total.

No population column

The WHO file has no populations in it, so nothing here is per-head. India and Iceland are compared on raw counts only, and rankings favour large countries by construction.

Weekly reporting, daily file

Most countries switched to weekly reporting during 2022, which lands a week of cases on a single date. That is why the daily series is spiky and why every trend here is drawn on a 7-day average.

Restatements

Countries occasionally revise their back-series, which shows up as a negative daily value. There are a few such days in this file. Totals come from WHO’s own cumulative column, so they stay consistent with the source.

The last day is not the end

This snapshot stops at , when the WHO wound down daily global reporting. A flat tail means reporting stopped, not that transmission did.

Case fatality rate

CFR here is reported deaths ÷ reported cases. Where testing was scarce it runs high; it is not the infection fatality rate and should not be read as one.
